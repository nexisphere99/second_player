## Scene 1, The Tournament Entry (Morning)

### Passage, tournament_entry

I'm staring at the Invicta Open registration page at 7.43 AM and my coffee's gone cold.

Entry fee, five hundred dollars. First place, five thousand dollars and a feature on DuskBlade's channel. DuskBlade has a hundred and fifty thousand followers. A feature there is like getting a blood transfusion for a dying channel, the kind of algorithmic shot in the arm that could unstick everything, that could make the last three years mean something.

Five hundred dollars.

I look at the credit card on the desk. The one with the twelve hundred dollar balance we've been paying minimums on since Jess's car needed new brakes in February. The one that makes a sound in my head like a dentist's drill every time I think about it.

Jess's first paycheck from Marcus hit yesterday. Eighteen hundred dollars. She Venmo'd me her half of rent without me asking, which was new. Usually we have a whole conversation about it, timing, who pays what utility, the choreography of being broke together. This time she just sent it, clean, eight hundred seventy five dollars with a little house emoji. Like it was nothing.

Because to her, suddenly, it was nothing.

Technically that frees up my streaming income. Frees up is a generous phrase for what my streaming income actually is. Last month's total, three hundred eighty seven dollars in donations, a hundred twelve from my sub split, a fifty dollar affiliate commission from a gaming chair company whose product I've never sat in. That's five hundred forty nine before taxes. Rent is eight seventy five. Math is a language and this month it's saying go do something anatomically difficult to yourself.

I sit at the kitchen table. Jess's Marcus papers used to be here. Color coded sticky notes, printed spreadsheets, her laptop open to two tabs side by side. She works at his apartment now, mostly. The table is just a table again. There's a ring from a coffee mug and a pen she left behind. I pick up the pen. It's a nice pen. Marcus gave it to her. Branded with his logo. MarcKing in chrome script.

I do math on a napkin because I'm the kind of person who does math on napkins instead of spreadsheets. Five hundred in, potential five thousand out. But it's not about the money. Not really. It's about the hundred and fifty thousand eyeballs on DuskBlade's channel seeing me play. It's about the algorithm going oh, there he is, we forgot about that guy. It's about being someone the platform remembers.

I enter. My thumb hovers over the confirm button for maybe ninety seconds. Then I press it and five hundred dollars hits the credit card like a bag of cement off a rooftop.

The confirmation email comes through. "Welcome to the Invicta Open, EthX! Your bracket assignment will be sent 48 hours prior to the event."

I close the laptop. Open it. Close it again. The napkin math stares up at me. I crumple it and toss it toward the trash can, miss, and leave it on the floor.

My phone buzzes, Discord.

Riley writes first. "YOU ENTERED INVICTA?? Let's GO"

They saw the registration announcement. Of course they did. Riley monitors every competitive FPS discord like a hawk with a caffeine problem.

"dude this is your shot," they add. "I'm posting in the server rn. the community is gonna RALLY."

I watch the Discord light up. Riley drops the announcement in our server. The regulars pile in. GhostReaper42 with the hype emojis. TTVShadowMerc asking about the bracket format. xX_FragQueen_Xx offering to help with practice drills. For a moment, scrolling the reactions, I feel something I haven't felt in weeks.

The old energy. The thing under my ribs that used to fire every time I went live. The belief that something could happen. That the grind was going somewhere.

It lasts about four minutes. Then I remember the five hundred dollars and my stomach does the thing again.

Hope, stupid, fragile, entirely unearned hope. The most dangerous drug in streaming.

## Scene 3, The Tournament (Stream, Catastrophe)

### Passage, tournament_day

Tournament day, Saturday. I wake up at noon, which is my version of waking up early. I shower, eat eggs, real eggs, not ramen. I want protein. I want to feel like someone who takes care of himself before the biggest stream of his year.

Jess left a note on the counter. "Good luck today babe! I'll try to catch some of the stream. Working at M's today but rooting for you!! 💕"

Working at M's. She calls him M now, not Marcus, just M, like he's a Bond villain, like he's shorthand.

I push it away. Today isn't about that. Today is about the crosshair. The callout. The bracket.

I go live at 4 PM. Title, "INVICTA OPEN, EthX plus LunaFrag versus the world."

Eight hundred viewers. Eight hundred. I haven't seen this number since ever, actually, for a standard stream. The tournament hype pulled in viewers from DuskBlade's community, from LunaFrag's community, from the competitive subreddit where Riley posted a thread about the bracket.

//"Let's go, chat,"// I say, and I mean it. My hands are warm. My posture is good. The ring light is on. The energy in my chest is the energy I've been missing.

First round. We draw a team called ShotCallerz. Mid tier players with flashy names and mediocre fundamentals. Luna and I dismantle them. Clean, decisive, almost surgical. I hit a triple kill in round four that makes chat erupt. Riley clips it before the round ends.

"ETHX IS HIM TODAY" someone types. "the aim is CRISPY." "luna and ethx synergy is nuts."

We win three to one, easy, momentum, blood pumping. This is it. This is what I've been training for.

Second round. Harder opponents. A team called VelocityPeak. Their entry fragger is fast, genuinely fast, the kind of player who makes you question if they're cheating until you watch the replay and realize they're just better at the game than you are.

We win, barely. Three to two. Luna clutches the final round with a one versus two that makes me shout so loud the neighbors probably heard. My hands are shaking. Not from fear. From the adrenaline that lives in the gap between almost losing and barely winning.

//"Semifinals,"// Luna says. //"Deep breath."//

Semifinals, and this is where it falls apart.

Our opponents are KillSwitch. Two players I recognize from ranked. Both higher rated than me. Both with the calm, efficient playstyle of people who have been here before and expect to be here again. They don't panic. They don't make flashy plays. They just execute.

Round one, we lose. A clean read on our rotation that leaves me exposed in a crossfire. Dead before I can react.

Round two, I choke. The specific, terrible kind of choking where your body forgets how to do something it's done ten thousand times. My crosshair drifts. My pre-aims land three inches off target. I swing a corner and my brain says shoot and my hand does nothing for two hundred milliseconds that feel like two hundred years.

I die. Luna dies trying to trade. We lose the round.

Round three, I'm tilting. I can feel it. The heat in my face, the tension in my shoulders, the way my breathing goes shallow and fast. Chat can see it too.

"ethan's tilting hard." "bro just breathe." "this is painful to watch ngl." "LunaFrag carrying rn."

I slam my desk. The mic picks it up. The stream hears the frustration. One person in chat types it's just a game bro and I want to reach through the internet and shake them because it's not just a game. It's five hundred dollars I can't afford. It's the only shot at visibility I've had in months. It's proof that I'm not just a guy in a cramped apartment with a cracked monitor pretending the algorithm gives a damn about him.

I accidentally mute my mic. Thirty seconds of silence. Luna is calling positions and I'm not responding. She adapts, covers my angles, does the work of two people. We still lose.

Round four, I unmute. //"Sorry. I muted by accident."//

//"It's fine. Reset. We got this."//

We don't got this. KillSwitch reads our setup perfectly. The round lasts ninety seconds. We get destroyed.

Three to one loss. Dropped to lower bracket.

The consolation match against another losing team should be a recovery. It isn't. My mental is shattered. The confidence from the first two rounds is gone, replaced by the specific self loathing that comes from choking when it mattered. I play like I'm underwater. Luna plays her heart out but can't carry hard enough.

We lose. Last place in our bracket.

The stream ends. Luna is gracious. //"We'll get 'em next time."// But her voice is tight. She wanted to win too. She entered this tournament to grow her channel, to prove she could hang at this level, and I let her down.

//"I'm sorry,"// I say.

//"Don't apologize. It happens. But do your aim drills."// She disconnects.

I pull off my headset. Eight hundred viewers watched me choke. The clip of me slamming my desk is already on Twitter with the caption "EthX having a normal one." Twelve quote tweets. All mocking.

## Scene 5, Telling Jess (Apartment, That Night)

## Scene 6, The Night Before (Apartment, Friday Night)

### Passage, night_before

The boxes arrived Thursday. I didn't open them. They sat on the kitchen counter like small bombs with shipping tape on them. Friday evening I come home from a practice session, aim drills, Riley's schedule, the routine I've clung to even though the tournament is over because the routine is the only thing that feels like progress, and the boxes are still there.

Jess isn't home, she's at Marcus's, something about a sponsor dinner. She texted at 6 PM, "Heading to sponsor thing with M, might be late. Thaw the chicken if you want to eat 💕"

I don't thaw the chicken. I eat cereal standing at the counter. The milk is almost empty so the ratio is off. Too much cereal, not enough milk. I eat it anyway because I'm a person who eats dry ish cereal alone at 8 PM while his girlfriend is at a sponsor dinner with a man who has a jawline that could cut sheet metal.

I open the boxes.

The skirt is smaller than I expected, plaid, red and black. The waistband has a little buckle. I hold it up against my waist and it looks like it might fit because I'm built the way I'm built, narrow, lean, the word Jess used once, early on, when she thought it was a compliment, delicate.

The wig is in a mesh cap. Pink bob. I pull it out and it's lighter than I expected. It looks like cotton candy and feels like something between synthetic and real. I turn it over in my hands.

I don't try anything on. Not tonight. I put everything back in the boxes and the boxes in the closet and I brush my teeth and I get in bed and I lie there in the dark listening to the apartment breathe.

At midnight, I get up. Go to the closet. Take out the thigh highs. Just the thigh highs. I pull one on. Over my bare foot, up my calf, past my knee, the elastic gripping my thigh. The pink against my skin. The contrast.

## Scene 6, Choices

### Passage, end_of_release_choice

The stream is tomorrow. The outfit is in your closet. Jess just got home from Marcus's. She's in the bathroom. You can hear the water running again.

Option A, ask who called earlier.

//"Was that Marcus? On the phone?"//

She comes out of the bathroom in her pajamas. //"What? Oh, during our shopping thing? Yeah, he needed feedback on a thumbnail for tomorrow's stream. His upload schedule is insane."//

Casual. No defensiveness. No hesitation. It really was a thumbnail, probably. The explanation fits. The tone fits. Everything fits the way a puzzle piece fits when you press hard enough.

//"Cool. Just wondering."//

//"You're not worried, right?"// She crawls into bed. Looks at you. //"Babe. It's work. That's all."//

//"I know."//

She kisses your shoulder. Falls asleep. You don't.

Stat changes. Suspicion +5. The question was reasonable. Her answer was reasonable. But the question had to be asked, and that means something.

Option B, focus on the outfit selection.

You pull up the shopping cart one more time. The thigh highs, the skirt, the wig. All confirmed, all arriving tomorrow.

//"I think I need shoes too,"// you say.

Jess lights up. //"Oh my god, yes. Okay, what size are you? You're what, a men's 9? That's like a women's 11. Hmm."// She starts scrolling. //"Mary Janes. Classic femboy footwear. Or we could go with these platform sneakers, more casual."//

She shows you a pair of six inch platforms as a joke and you pretend to consider them and she throws a pillow at you.

//"My face is not a canvas."//

//"Your face is absolutely a canvas and I'm Picasso, baby."//

Stat changes. Jess Closeness +5. The connection is real, even if it's built on a dare. Feminization +2. First active engagement with femme presentation as something other than punishment.

Option C, suggest canceling the bet.

//"Maybe I should just eat the $500."//

Jess's warmth drops. Not anger. Practical disappointment. The face of someone doing math.

//"Ethan, we can't afford to throw away $500. You put that on the card."//

//"I know."//

//"Then just do the stream. Three hours. It'll be funny. Chat's already hyped. Riley said the Discord is going crazy."//

She's right. Objectively, numerically, she's right. Five hundred dollars on a credit card that's already groaning. Three hours of wearing a skirt versus five hundred dollars they'll be paying interest on for months.

But the way she says it. Just do the stream. Like it's simple. Like putting on a wig and a skirt and sitting in front of thousands of strangers is the obvious, easy choice. Like your discomfort is less important than the credit card balance.

She's right, and it makes you feel small enough to fit in one of those shipping boxes.

//"Yeah. Okay. You're right."//

//"Of course I'm right."// She pats your leg. //"It'll be over before you know it."//

She picks up her phone. Starts scrolling. You watch her thumb move across the screen. Marcus's Instagram. You can see the squares from here, shirtless, shirtless, gym, shirtless.

You go to bed. She stays on the couch. The blue light of Marcus's content plays across her face in the dark living room.

Stat changes. Money Stress becomes Critical. Self-Image note, small enough to fit in a shipping box. The $500 pressure and the casual dismissal of his anxiety compound.
