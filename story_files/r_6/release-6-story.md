"Ethan, you actually look."

### Passage, R6_JessDrift

Jess is spending three evenings a week at Marcus's apartment now.

I count. Monday, Wednesday, Friday sometimes, sometimes Thursday. The schedule rotates but the frequency is fixed. Three nights out of seven, the apartment is mine, not ours, mine.

On those nights I develop rituals I don't tell anyone about. Cook dinner for one. Eat at the counter because the table feels too big for one person, stream, check her Instagram stories between matches. Cook more food she won't eat. Leave it in the fridge with a sticky note. She'll heat it up at midnight or she won't.

9 PM, "Still going, this deck is huge." The update. The reassurance. The I haven't forgotten you exist reminder that shouldn't need to exist in a relationship where someone actually remembers you exist.

10 PM, "Running late, Marcus wants to reshoot some promo stuff. Don't wait up ❤️" The closer. The heart emoji. That heart emoji.

She sends it the way you sign a form letter. The way you write best regards on an email to someone you've never met, automatic. The heart isn't love. The heart is habit. Muscle memory. A reflex left over from the version of Jess who used to text me can't wait to come home to you and mean every letter.

I sit on the couch and the apartment smells like garlic from the chicken I made that's cooling in the fridge. I open Instagram.

Marcus posted twenty minutes ago. His apartment. The kitchen. The good kitchen, the one with the marble counters and the pendant lights and the subway tile backsplash that costs more than my entire apartment's security deposit. He's got takeout containers on the counter. Two of them. Thai food, from the look of it. Two sets of chopsticks laid out side by side. Behind the containers, a bottle of red wine. Half empty. Two glasses.

Two containers. Two chopsticks. Two glasses. He posted this at 8 PM.

At 8 PM Jess texted me that she was still working on a huge deck.

I zoom in. The wine glasses are different sizes. One is larger, a standard pour. The other is smaller, more delicate. The kind of glass you'd choose if you had small hands. Jess has small hands.

I zoom in more. Behind the wine bottle, just at the edge of the frame, there's a jacket draped over a barstool. I can't see the whole thing. Just the sleeve, denim, a shade of denim I recognize the way you recognize your own handwriting.

Something I don't want to name starts tightening in my chest. A fist closing around something that used to be certainty.

She comes home at 11. I'm on the couch pretending to watch a show I stopped following twenty minutes ago. The front door opens and she comes in and I smell it before I see her, different, not wrong, just different. A body wash that isn't the one in our shower. Something woodsy and clean where ours is citrus.

//"Yeah?"//

//"Marcus's sponsor deck was a nightmare. Fourteen slides, all needed rework."// She's in the kitchen getting water. Not looking at me. //"But I think we nailed it."//

//"That's great."//

//"Oh, I started going to the gym, by the way,"// she says, casual, like she's mentioning a weather change. //"Marcus's gym. His trainer offered me a free trial membership."//

Marcus's gym. Marcus's trainer. Marcus's body wash, probably, from the gym shower.

//"That's cool,"// I say.

//"Yeah. It feels good to move, you know? I've been sitting at a desk too much."//

She showers before bed. She usually showers in the morning. Has for the entire three years I've known her. Morning shower person. But tonight she showers at 11.15 PM. The water runs for a long time and I lie in bed and listen to it and count and tell myself I'm not counting.

Twelve minutes, thirteen, fourteen.

//"Night, babe."//

//"Night."//

She's asleep in four minutes, fast, the kind of fast that comes from a body that's already been spent. The kind of sleep you fall into after a long workout, or after good sex, or after three hours of pretending to be someone's manager when you're becoming something else.

I lie awake. Stare at the ceiling crack. Think about two wine glasses and a jacket on a barstool. Think about eight forty seven dollars and a pink wig. Think about the word or that Riley left hanging.

The phone on my nightstand glows with another notification. I don't look at it. I look at the ceiling.

The crack hasn't changed. Everything else has.

### Final Player Choice, The Decision

Option A, accept the sponsor deal.
I type the reply at 11 PM. "Dana, I'm in. Let's do this." Send. The cursor blinks for a second after the message goes. A held breath. Then, done, committed. At least two EthX-chan streams per month. Professional wigs. Real money. A persona that isn't a punishment anymore. It's a career decision. The LunaLocks package arrives three days later. Three wigs in a branded box with tissue paper and care instructions. The chestnut brown one is even prettier in person. I hold it in my hands and it feels like real hair and it weighs almost nothing and it cost more than every piece of clothing I own combined. I put it on in the bathroom, alone, no camera, no audience. Just me and the mirror. The chestnut waves frame my face and the person looking back is someone I'm going to have to get to know. "Hey," I say to her. My voice sounds stupid. I say it again anyway. "Hey." She doesn't answer. But she doesn't leave either.

Option B, decline and keep trying regular content.
I type the reply at 11 PM. "Dana, I appreciate the offer, but I'm going to pass for now. I want to focus on my competitive gaming content." Send. The cursor blinks. The rejection is polite and professional and it tastes like sawdust. I stream as EthX for the next week. Monday, two hundred fifty viewers. Wednesday, two hundred twenty. Friday, a hundred ninety. The decline is visible. A slope heading toward a number I don't want to think about. GhostReaper42 donates five dollars every stream. The only constant in a room that's emptying. The wig from the punishment stream is in a bag in the closet. I don't throw it away. I tell myself that's because it wasn't mine to throw away, it was a costume, it belongs to the bit. I tell myself a lot of things at 2 AM while the bag sits in the closet and the closet sits in the room and the room sits in an apartment that's getting quieter by the week.

Option C, confront Jess about her hours.
"You're never here anymore." The words come out before I plan them. Sunday night. She's on the couch. I'm in the kitchen doorway. The vanilla candle is burning between us like a tiny mediator. Her head snaps up. "What?" "You're never here. Three nights a week at Marcus's place. You come home smelling like someone else's shower. You stopped editing my highlights. You stopped watching my streams. You weren't even here for the biggest night of my career." "I'm working, Ethan. For us. You think managing Marcus is a vacation? You think I'm sitting around drinking wine at his apartment? I'm building a brand." The wine glasses flash through my mind. The takeout for two. The lipstick on the rim. "I'm not saying you're not working." "Then what are you saying?" I don't know what I'm saying. I know what I'm thinking but the thinking and the saying are different animals and only one of them is safe to release. "I just miss you," I say. The truest, smallest, most pathetic version of everything I'm feeling. Condensed into four words that don't even begin to cover it. Her face softens. For a second she's the Jess from three years ago. The one who would have crossed the room and held me. "I miss you too, babe. I'll be home more. I promise." She won't be. We both know she won't be. But the promise is warm and the candle smells like vanilla and for one night the apartment feels like it belongs to two people again.

### Passage, R6_ReleaseEnd

I lie in bed. Jess is beside me tonight. Her phone is charging on the nightstand, screen down. My phone is on mine, screen up, the LunaLocks catalog still open in a tab.

Between us, twelve inches of mattress. The same mattress from when we moved in together, a year and a half ago, when twelve inches meant we were close. Now twelve inches is a canyon. A border. A demilitarized zone.

I close my eyes. Behind them, five thousand people screaming. A pink wig. A reflection that looked happy. A jacket on a barstool. A wine glass with lipstick. A donation that said good girl. A friend who said whatever it is, it's okay. A sales rep who said take your time. A stranger on a forum who said sometimes the mask reveals the face.

Sleep comes slow. When it arrives, I dream about two people in a mirror. One of them is smiling.

I don't know which one is me.
