PLAYER TWO - RELEASE 1 - THE GRIND
Full Narrative Script (First-Person, Ethan "EthX" Marsh)

Writing style - confessional neo-noir, dirty realism (think early Chuck Palahniuk meets internet-age Bukowski). Short punchy sentences, raw internal monologue, no literary posturing, cuts between thought and action without ceremony.

## Start

The monitor hums. That's the first thing, before the chat loads, before the webcam light blinks green, before I paste on whatever version of myself tonight's audience gets. The hum gets into my teeth if I sit here long enough.

Cracked bezel, lower right corner. Six months I've been meaning to replace this thing. Six months of telling myself next paycheck, next sponsor deal, next big break. The crack runs diagonal across the corner like a fault line through my career.

I click Go Live.

"What's good, chat. EthX here. Let's get into it."

Two hundred faces, or two hundred usernames anyway. Maybe a hundred actual humans, the rest bots and lurkers and people who left the tab open while they went to take a shit. My ring light is clamped to a bookshelf because a real stand costs eighty bucks, and eighty bucks is two weeks of ramen for two people.

The LED strips paint everything purple. I bought them during a manic 3 AM impulse buy, back when I thought aesthetic could fix what skill couldn't. Twenty two dollars. The apartment looks like a low budget cyberpunk set, which tracks, because that's basically what it is.

I queue ranked. The matchmaking wheel spins. Chat trickles in.

TTVShadowMerc shows up first.
"yooo ethx whats good"

xX_FragQueen_Xx right behind him.
"early tonight king"

GhostReaper42 drops in a second later.
"lets get this bread"

Three regulars. The same three regulars who show up every stream like clockwork, like some kind of parasocial obligation they can't shake. I love them. I also want to ask them why, why here, why me, when there are ten thousand streamers doing exactly what I do and most of them have better equipment.

"Shadow, Frag, Ghost. The holy trinity. Let's run it."

Match starts. I drop clean. Crosshair placement is solid, movement is crisp, I hit a flick shot that in any other context would be clip worthy. Nobody clips it. Nobody clips anything anymore. My highlight reel is a graveyard of plays that deserved better than 180 concurrent viewers.

I check analytics on the second tab while I'm in queue between matches. I shouldn't. I always do.

Follower growth sits flat, a line so horizontal it looks like a heart monitor on a corpse.

Average watch time is declining too. Twenty two minutes last month. Nineteen this month. People are staying shorter, getting bored faster, finding the door quicker than they used to.

The algorithm stopped recommending me at some point I never pinpointed. There's no email, no notification, no dear EthX, you are no longer interesting enough for us to promote. It just happens. The suggestions tab forgets you exist and you become a ghost in a machine that only feeds the living.

"Nice shot, nice shot. Let's rotate left, team."

I'm performing enthusiasm I don't feel. The catchphrases that used to erupt on their own now come from a script I keep in my head. Let's get it. Clean play. Chat, we're cooking. Rehearsed, hollow, the laugh track of a show that's been canceled but nobody told the actors.

Ninety minutes pass. One donation shows up.

Five dollars from GhostReaper42, message attached. "nice shot king"

"Ghost. My man. Appreciate the five, brother. You're the real king."

I mean it, for about three seconds I genuinely mean it. The warmth cracks through the exhaustion like sunlight through a gap in a wall. Then it seals back up.

Riley's in the mod channel. They DM me mid stream.

Riley, kind lie and all. "Good energy tonight E, chat's vibing 👍"

I can feel the room's indifference the way you can feel humidity, everywhere, in every pause between chat messages. They're not vibing. They're waiting for something worth vibing about, and I'm not providing it tonight.

TTVShadowMerc types a question. "when's the next tourney?"

I give a vague answer, something about looking into options and might have something lined up. Translation, the tournaments that matter cost five hundred bucks to enter and I have three hundred and forty dollars in checking and rent is due in twelve days.

Midnight comes and I end the stream. Raid target, some kid with forty viewers playing the same game I was playing, worse than me but with a joy that makes me feel ancient at twenty four. I hit the raid and watch my 180 viewers flood his tiny channel like relatives showing up uninvited to a kid's lemonade stand.

I can't raid up anymore. That's the metric nobody talks about. When you raid, you're supposed to punch up, send your viewers to someone bigger, build alliances and networks. I used to raid five hundred viewer channels and they'd notice, thank me on stream, sometimes raid back. Now I raid downward, into the dark, into the forty viewer channels where nobody knows my name and my 180 viewers are a tsunami for maybe fifteen minutes before they drain away.

Headset off, dropped on the desk. The LED strips keep going because I'm too tired to reach the switch. Purple light and silence. The energy drink cans along the windowsill catch the glow, nine of them lined up, a small city of caffeine and desperation.

## The Apartment

I walk to the kitchen and the apartment walks with me. Three steps from desk to door, eight steps down the hallway, four steps to the fridge. I've counted them. When you live somewhere small enough to memorize in footsteps, you stop calling it home and start calling it what it actually is, a container.

One bedroom. The gaming setup sits in the corner, three feet from the bed. When I stream, Jess has to either sit quietly or leave the room. She used to sit on the bed with headphones on and work on my highlights. Now she leaves.

The kitchen is a galley, one person wide. Open the fridge while someone walks behind you and somebody's getting hip checked. The living room fits a couch, a TV, and the specific kind of sadness that comes from knowing this is your mid twenties and you thought it would look different by now.

Jess's part time retail schedule is pinned to the fridge with a cat shaped magnet. Color coded, because that's who Jess is. Blue for morning shifts, green for closings, red for requested off days. She requested off last Friday. I don't remember what we did with it. Probably nothing.

I heat up leftover ramen, the packet kind, not real ramen, the kind that costs thirty cents and tastes like salt and unfulfilled potential. Steam rises while I stand at the counter eating, because we only have two chairs and one of them has a wobbly leg I keep saying I'll fix.

My phone's in my other hand because I can't stand alone with my own silence anymore. I scroll through other streamers' highlight reels. Someone I used to scrim with just hit 50K followers. A girl I collabed with last year is doing sponsored content for an energy drink brand. Everybody's trajectory is a curve. Mine is a flatline.

The apartment smells like leftover ramen and the synthetic coconut of the air freshener Jess bought because she said the place smells like boy. She's not wrong. It smells like boy, like unwashed hoodies and energy drinks and the slow evaporation of ambition.

I finish the ramen, rinse the bowl, walk to the bedroom.

## The Bedroom

Jess is already in bed. Her laptop sits open beside her, the editing software paused mid timeline on one of my highlights, half cut. She was working on it, then she stopped. The progress bar sits at maybe forty percent, cursor blinking in the gap between two clips like it's waiting for permission to continue.

Her phone is in her hand instead. TikTok on autoplay, the screen throwing colors across her face. She's beautiful in that absent way people are when they don't know they're being watched. Brown hair fanned across the pillow, tank top, bare shoulders. Three years of my life curled up in this bed, and she still makes my chest tighten when I catch her unguarded.

She looks up.

"How'd the stream go?"

"Fine. One eighty average."

"That's solid."

It's not solid. She knows it, I know it, and we both let the word sit there, generous and hollow, because the alternative is saying one eighty is exactly where you were three months ago and three months before that, and the word for that isn't solid, it's stuck.

She goes back to TikTok. I go to the bathroom, brush my teeth, stare at myself in the mirror longer than is healthy. Soft jaw. Eyes that look tired even when I'm not. Features my mom once called pretty when I was fourteen, and I spent the next decade trying to bulk past that word.

I slide into bed. She's warm, always runs warm, like a space heater in human form. I press my lips to the back of her neck, the familiar opener, three years of the same move. She tilts her head, giving me access, and the gesture is generous the same way her solid was generous, allowing rather than pursuing.

## Intimate (Mild)

I kiss down her neck. My hand slides along her hip. She makes a small sound, an mm, and her body stays where it is, facing away. She doesn't turn toward me.

I go down on her. Pull the sheets back, settle between her thighs. She's warm here too, warm everywhere, and she opens for me with the practiced ease of a thousand identical nights. I lick slow, broad strokes, the technique I learned in year one that used to make her gasp.

She doesn't gasp now. She breathes, steady, present but not consumed. No hands in my hair, no fingers gripping the pillow, no whispered instructions. I'm performing, she's receiving, and somewhere between mouth and flesh there's a gap that didn't used to be there.

I come back up. She opens her legs wider, a permission rather than a demand. I push in and she's wet enough, warm enough, tight enough, all the physical qualifiers present and accounted for. Her legs wrap around me, loosely, the hold of someone who's along for the ride, not driving it.

I move. The rhythm is familiar, automatic, like a song you've played so many times your fingers know the chords but your brain has left the building. Her breath hitches once and I chase that sound, try to find it again, but it was a random fluctuation, not a pattern.

Five minutes pass, maybe less. I feel it building and there's nothing poetic about it, no fireworks, just the mechanical inevitability of friction and biology. I finish with my forehead against her shoulder, breathing into her skin, smelling her shampoo, coconut and something herbal, the same shampoo since year one.

"Mmmh," she exhales beneath me.

"That was nice," she says.

Her phone is already back in her hand. I'm still inside her and she's reaching for the screen. The glow lights up the space between her face and the ceiling. TikTok, some video of a dog doing something stupid, tinny audio playing while I soften and slip out and roll to my side.

I lie on my back, staring at the ceiling. The purple LED from the desk paints shadows across the popcorn texture.

My PC pings. New follower notification, one, a single new follower from tonight's stream. One person out of the millions scrolling through Twitch decided that what I do is worth a follow button.

Just one. The number feels almost deliberate, like the universe is being specific about how much I matter.

I used to make her grab the headboard. She'd pull me in with her heels on my back and call me baby and come so hard she'd go silent, just this open mouth nothing sound, then laugh after, this breathy amazed laugh, and say holy shit like she'd surprised herself.

When did she start checking her phone before I'd even pulled out.

He's laughing about something, genuinely laughing. At midnight, in a studio apartment, with twelve viewers, Dale is having the time of his life.

I stand in my kitchen in my boxers and listen to a man enjoying the thing I've turned into a job.

## Side Quest - The Gym Encounter

I should go to the gym. I haven't gone in three weeks. The membership runs thirty bucks a month, which is thirty bucks I feel guilty about every time the charge hits, but canceling it feels like admitting something I'm not ready to admit. So I pay for a gym I don't go to, because the idea of going is worth thirty dollars and the reality of going requires energy I've already spent streaming.

Today I actually go, force of will, or maybe force of guilt. Either way I'm in basketball shorts and a hoodie walking three blocks to Planet Fitness at 11 AM on a Tuesday.

The gym is mostly empty, a few retirees on treadmills, a mom doing bicep curls with five pound dumbbells while her toddler sleeps in a stroller, and one guy on the bench press I recognize from his Instagram before I recognize his face.

Marcus "MarcKing" Reeves.

He's benching 225, easy reps, the bar moving like it weighs nothing. He's in a cutoff that shows arms the circumference of my thighs, sweat gleaming on dark skin. AirPods in, nodding to whatever he's listening to, phone propped against his water bottle, recording.

Of course he's recording. Content is oxygen for people like Marcus. Every rep is potential footage, every bead of sweat a thumbnail waiting to happen.

I grab dumbbells, thirties, my usual. I curl them in the mirror and try not to look at him. I fail. In the reflection we're a split screen of two trajectories. His body takes up space like it has a right to. Mine occupies space like it's apologizing for being there.

He catches me looking, smiles, that smile I've seen on a thousand clips and it hits different in person. It's not performed. It's reflexive, like smiling at people is his resting state.

"Hey, bro. You're EthX, right? I've seen your ranked clips. Your crosshair placement is nasty."

He knows my name. Marcus Reeves, six hundred thousand followers, knows my name. The fact that this matters to me is its own kind of pathetic.

## Evening Stream (Day 2)

Second stream of the release. Same setup, same purple light, same cracked bezel. The follower count reads 80,001. That single follower from last night, preserved in the number like a fossil.

I go live at 8 PM. Riley's modding. The usuals trickle in, 190 tonight, ten more than average, which is either statistical noise or the beginning of something. Probably noise.

I'm playing ranked again. The gameplay is sharper tonight, whether it's the coffee or the LunaFrag email or the residual adrenaline of standing within arm's reach of Marcus Reeves at the gym, I'm hitting shots that feel important, flicks that snap, crosshair placement that anticipates rather than reacts.

Nobody clips it. The plays dissolve into the VOD, uncaptured, unseen except by the 190 people who happen to be watching at this specific second on this specific night.

Chat is doing its thing. TTVShadowMerc theorycrafting loadouts, FragQueen backseat gaming my rotations, GhostReaper dropping another five dollars. "goated as always king." Riley keeps the peace when a random troll wanders in with a bad take about my aim.

I read donations, do my bits, say let's get it and we're cooking and clean play, clean play. The words come out and they sound like me and they also sound like a recording of me, and I can't tell the difference anymore.

Two hours in, a notification pops on my second monitor. Not Twitch, Instagram. Marcus posted a story.

I shouldn't check it mid stream. I check it mid stream.

Marcus is streaming, shirtless, horror game. The clip that autoplays shows him flinching at a jump scare, his entire torso doing something involuntary and muscular. His laugh after is deep and rolling and genuine, and chat is moving so fast it looks like the Matrix.

The donation ticker in his overlay hasn't stopped moving, not once in the twenty second clip, an unbroken scroll of money and adoration.

I close Instagram, return to my game, hit a nice triple kill that chat appreciates with moderate enthusiasm.

"Let's gooo. EthX gaming."

190 viewers. The number sits in the corner of my screen like a grade I didn't study for.

## Post-Stream Quiet

Stream ends at eleven thirty, didn't make it to midnight tonight. The energy ran out. I blamed it on an early morning tomorrow, which is a lie, because I don't have mornings. I have the same unstructured void I have every day, stretching from wake to stream to sleep.

Eight dollars in donations tonight, GhostReaper's five and a random three from someone named ttv_brokeboi69, message attached. "ur underrated fr." The irony of the username is not lost on me.

Jess isn't home yet. She texted at 9 PM. "Staying late to close. Manager called in sick. Ugh."

The apartment is dark except for the LEDs. I sit in the glow and eat cold pizza from the box on the counter, two slices, the cheese congealed into something that requires commitment to chew.

I should shower. I should go to bed. I should do a lot of things.

Instead I sit at my desk and watch Marcus's VOD, the full stream, three hours, skipping through it, stopping at moments, the jump scares, the ASMR segments where he leans into the mic and murmurs things that make my skin prickle even through laptop speakers, the donation reads where he says every person's name like it matters.

He's good, that's the thing. If he were a fraud, a gimmick, a pretty face with nothing behind it, I could hate him cleanly. But he's good, funny, his timing impeccable. The shirtless thing isn't the whole brand, it's the hook, and what keeps people is the actual human being behind the abs.

I close the VOD at 1 AM. Jess's key turns in the door.

"Hey. Long night?"

"The worst. Janet called in again and I had to solo close."

She kicks off her shoes, drops her bag. She looks tired in a way that's different from my tired. Hers is physical, feet hurt, back sore, retail tired. Mine is existential. The difference matters but we never talk about it, because comparing tiredness is a competition nobody wins.

She microwaves a slice of pizza, eats it standing. We're parallel structures in a galley kitchen, two people who love each other eating bad food in a small space and not saying the things that would change everything.

"I'm beat," she says. "Coming to bed?"

"In a minute."

She goes. I stay. The monitor hums.

## Release 1 End

I lie in bed, Jess beside me, the ceiling above me, the life around me.

Somewhere in this city Marcus Reeves is sleeping in an apartment with marble counters and a streaming setup that costs more than my car. Somewhere, six hundred thousand people follow his name. Somewhere, the algorithm is deciding who matters tomorrow, and my name isn't in the running.

But 80,001 people know I exist. One more than yesterday.

I close my eyes. Tomorrow I'll stream again, tomorrow I'll try again, tomorrow Jess will go to work and come home and we'll eat something cheap and watch something free and go to bed in a room where the monitor hums three feet from the pillow.

This is the grind. Not glamorous, not tragic, just the specific relentless mediocrity of almost making it.

The LED strips paint the ceiling purple. I watch the color until my eyes close.
